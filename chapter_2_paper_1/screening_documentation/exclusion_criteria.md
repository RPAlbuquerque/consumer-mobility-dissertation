# Exclusion Criteria

## Purpose

The exclusion criteria document the primary reasons why records screened for the systematic-integrative review were not retained. Each of the 599 unique records received one final decision, and every excluded record was assigned one primary exclusion code.

## Records removed before screening

The 212 duplicate records removed after merging the Scopus and Web of Science exports were not classified through the substantive exclusion codes. Deduplication used normalized DOI as the primary key and normalized title plus publication year as a fallback.

The English-language journal-article requirement and the selected database subject-area filters were applied when the records were exported. These database restrictions defined the screening corpus and were not counted among the 513 substantive exclusions.

## Application of the codes

### R1: No relevant mobility or place construct

This code was assigned when mobility, visitation, location, place, or access did not perform a substantive role in the article. Geographic terminology, a location variable, or a passing reference to mobility did not establish eligibility when the study did not examine movement, visitation, spatial access, or place as part of its theoretical or empirical question.

### R2: No relevant market-context construct

This code was assigned when the article examined mobility or place but did not address a consumer, market, commercial, service, or local-market phenomenon. Studies concerned exclusively with technical, infrastructural, environmental, health, transportation, or administrative questions were excluded under this code when they lacked a relevant market context.

### R3: Insufficient behavioral or commercial connection

This code was assigned when the article included relevant mobility or place evidence and a market context but did not connect them sufficiently with consumer exposure, purchase behavior, spending, sales, commercial performance, or another behavioral or commercial result relevant to the review.

The use of *consumer exposure* in this screening criterion reflects the broader vocabulary required to assess the retrieved records. It does not make market exposure a construct in the final synthesis.

### R4: Insufficient transferability to the review question

This code was assigned to adjacent studies whose theory, evidence, measurement approach, or method did not transfer sufficiently to the review question. Examples included technical mobility modeling, transportation, tourism, health, sustainability, logistics, infrastructure, smart-city, or computing research whose findings could not substantively inform the interpretation of consumer mobility and local sales.

## Decision rules

- Each screened record received one final decision: include or exclude.
- Every excluded record received one primary code from `R1` through `R4`.
- When more than one limitation could apply, the screening record documented the primary reason supporting the final decision.
- Mobility, retail, spatial, or commercial terminology did not independently establish eligibility.
- Adjacent studies were retained only when their theoretical, empirical, measurement, or methodological contribution transferred substantively to the review question.
- Records used provisionally during screening calibration were resolved before the final counts were produced.
- No unresolved category remained in the final screening record.
