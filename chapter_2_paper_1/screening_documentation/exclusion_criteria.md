# Inclusion Criteria

## Purpose

The inclusion criteria defined which records could contribute to the systematic-integrative review of what existing research establishes about observed consumer mobility and local sales. The screening scope was intentionally broader than the final research question because relevant evidence appears in studies of physical presence, visitation, access, purchasing, spending, competition, commercial performance, and spatially heterogeneous markets.

## Database and publication scope

Before title, abstract, and keyword screening, the database filters limited the exported records to:

- English-language journal articles;
- records indexed in Scopus or Web of Science by May 15, 2026;
- no start-date restriction; and
- the subject areas and database categories documented in the search strategy.

These publication filters defined the searchable corpus but did not independently establish substantive eligibility.

## Required eligibility criteria

An article was retained only when it met all three predefined conditions.

### Criterion 1: Relevant mobility or place evidence

The article had to examine mobility, visitation, location, place, or access in a substantively relevant way.

Eligible evidence included, but was not limited to:

- consumer or customer mobility;
- pedestrian traffic or footfall;
- store entry, visits, or revisits;
- customer or visitor flows;
- movement within commercial environments;
- mobile-location, GPS, Wi-Fi, sensor, or point-of-interest records;
- spatial access, proximity, trade areas, catchments, or commercial areas; and
- other measures showing how consumers reached, moved through, or used places.

The presence of geographic terminology or mobility data alone did not establish eligibility when movement, visitation, place, or access was not substantively connected with the study’s question.

### Criterion 2: Relevant market context

The article had to address a consumer, market, commercial, service, or local-market phenomenon.

Eligible contexts included physical and digital channels, stores, services, shopping centers, commercial districts, trade areas, destinations, firms, categories, or other settings in which consumer movement could be related to behavioral or commercial evidence.

Retail terminology alone did not establish eligibility when the study did not address a relevant consumer, market, commercial, service, or local-market phenomenon.

### Criterion 3: Behavioral, commercial, or transferable theoretical connection

The article had to connect mobility or place with at least one of the following:

- consumer exposure;
- store entry or another behavior following arrival;
- purchase behavior;
- transaction incidence or frequency;
- spending;
- sales, revenue, profit, or commercial performance;
- the allocation of activity across firms, channels, categories, or places; or
- theory or measurement logic that transferred substantively to the interpretation of mobility and local sales across spatially heterogeneous markets.

The use of *exposure*, *opportunity*, or *conversion* in this criterion reflects the broad vocabulary needed to screen the literature and does not make these terms constructs in the final synthesis.

## Contribution to the review question

In addition to satisfying the three eligibility criteria, each retained article had to help answer at least one of the review’s five analytical questions:

1. Who was observed?
2. Why did consumers move, or what process generated the observed movement?
3. Which commercial outcome was measured?
4. Which firms, channels, and places were included?
5. What could the research design establish?

Articles could contribute through empirical findings, theory, measurement, or research-design logic, but the contribution had to transfer directly to the interpretation of observed consumer mobility and local sales.

## Decision rules

- All three substantive eligibility criteria had to be satisfied.
- An article could contribute to one or several analytical questions.
- Studies from adjacent fields were eligible when their evidence, theory, measurement, or design transferred substantively to the review question.
- The presence of mobility data, spatial terminology, marketing language, or a commercial setting did not independently guarantee inclusion.
- Records were excluded when the connection among mobility or place, the market context, and behavioral or commercial evidence was insufficient.
- Each excluded record received one primary exclusion reason according to the hierarchy documented in the screening protocol.
- The retained corpus consisted of 86 articles selected from 599 unique records.
