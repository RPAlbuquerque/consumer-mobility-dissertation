# Inclusion Criteria

An article was retained when it satisfied all required criteria and contributed substantively to the review question.

## Required criteria

1. The record was an English-language journal article within the database-export scope.
2. The article examined a mobility, visitation, location, place, or access construct, including consumer mobility, foot traffic, visits, customer flows, location traces, point-of-interest activity, spatial access, proximity, trade areas, or catchments.
3. The article addressed a consumer, customer, marketing, service, commercial, retail, or local-market phenomenon.
4. The article helped explain or measure at least one part of the relationship among consumer mobility, market exposure, localized market opportunity, local conversion conditions, and local sales.

## Contribution criteria

Eligible articles contributed to one or more of the following:

- conceptualization of consumer mobility as observed movement, visitation, proximity, or flow across or within places;
- operationalization of mobility-based evidence through foot traffic, visits, location traces, sensors, customer flows, point-of-interest activity, or spatial access;
- explanation of how observed mobility can create potential contact with focal offerings, services, commercial areas, channels, or place-based information;
- connection of mobility or market exposure with purchases, spending, transactions, sales, revenue, or other behavioral margins relevant to local sales;
- explanation of spatial, informational, competitive, commercial, environmental, or policy conditions associated with variation in how mobility relates to local sales; or
- transferable theory or measurement logic concerning store choice, trade areas, accessibility, commercial districts, place-based consumption, or spatially heterogeneous local markets.

Articles from adjacent fields were eligible when their evidence or theory transferred substantively to the marketing problem examined in the review. Retail language or the use of mobility data alone did not guarantee inclusion.
