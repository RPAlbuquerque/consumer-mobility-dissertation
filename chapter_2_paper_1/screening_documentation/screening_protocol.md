# Screening Protocol

## Research question

> What does existing research establish about the relationship between observed consumer mobility and local sales, and how do market settings, measures, and study designs shape what can be concluded?

## Screening unit

The screening unit was the unique bibliographic record remaining after the Scopus and Web of Science exports were merged and deduplicated.

Normalized DOI served as the primary deduplication key, while normalized article title plus publication year provided the fallback when a usable DOI was unavailable. This procedure removed 212 duplicate records, including 210 DOI matches and two title-year matches, leaving 599 unique records for screening.

## Screening responsibility and platform

The first author used Rayyan to organize the 599 unique records and document decisions while screening article titles, abstracts, and keywords. Rayyan supported record management and documentation, while the researcher made all inclusion and exclusion decisions.

Records used provisionally during screening calibration were resolved before the final counts were produced, leaving no unresolved category in the reported corpus.

## Eligibility logic

An article was retained only when it met all three predefined substantive conditions:

1. It examined mobility, visitation, location, place, or access in a substantively relevant way.
2. It addressed a consumer, market, commercial, service, or local-market phenomenon.
3. It connected mobility or place with consumer exposure, purchase behavior, spending, sales, commercial performance, or transferable theory for spatially heterogeneous markets.

The screening protocol was intentionally broader than the final wording of the research question because relevant evidence appears across studies of physical presence, visitation, purchasing, spending, accessibility, competition, and other commercial outcomes.

Retail terminology, mobility data, geographic language, or the presence of a commercial setting did not independently establish eligibility. Each record had to satisfy the three-part decision logic and contribute substantively to the interpretation of observed consumer mobility and local sales.

## Screening procedure

Each of the 599 unique records was evaluated through the following sequence:

1. Confirm that mobility, visitation, location, place, or access played a substantive role.
2. Confirm that the article addressed a relevant consumer, market, commercial, service, or local-market phenomenon.
3. Evaluate whether the article connected mobility or place with behavioral or commercial evidence or provided theory or measurement logic that transferred to the review question.
4. Record a final include or exclude decision.
5. For excluded records, assign one primary exclusion code and document the corresponding subtype, reason, and screening note.
6. For included articles, obtain and read the full text before coding the evidence used in the integrative synthesis.

## Final decisions

Each unique record received one final decision: include or exclude.

| Final decision | Records |
|---|---:|
| Included in the integrative synthesis | 86 |
| Excluded after screening | 513 |
| Total unique records screened | 599 |

Excluded records received one primary code from `R1` through `R4`, as defined in [`exclusion_criteria.md`](exclusion_criteria.md):

- `R1`: No relevant mobility or place construct.
- `R2`: No relevant market-context construct.
- `R3`: Insufficient behavioral or commercial connection.
- `R4`: Insufficient transferability to the review question.

The final screening record documented the include or exclude decision, primary exclusion code, subtype, reason, and screening note for every excluded record.

## Relationship with the integrative synthesis

The 86 retained articles were read in full and compared through the analytical structure documented in [`../coding_framework/coding_framework.md`](../coding_framework/coding_framework.md). Each article helped answer at least one of five questions:

1. Who was observed?
2. Why did consumers move, or what process generated the observed movement?
3. Which commercial outcome was measured?
4. Which firms, channels, and places were included?
5. What could the research design establish?

The synthesis preserved differences in concepts, measures, data sources, geographic support, units of analysis, outcomes, periods, market boundaries, and research designs. Visits were not treated as purchases, sales recorded by one organization were not treated as growth in the broader market, and predictive relationships were not interpreted as the effects of a specific action.

## Public reporting

The public repository reports the database queries, filters, deduplication rules, eligibility criteria, screening counts, aggregate exclusion records, coding protocol, and retained-study inventory. Commercial database exports, individual screening records, and copyrighted article files are not redistributed.

The screening process is reported according to PRISMA 2020, while the comparison of heterogeneous evidence follows the systematic-integrative design described in Chapter 2 of the dissertation.
