# Screening Protocol

## Research question

> Under what local conditions does observed consumer mobility relate to local sales?

## Screening unit

The screening unit was the unique bibliographic record remaining after deduplication of the Scopus and Web of Science exports.

## Screening logic

Each unique record was assessed against the eligibility criteria to determine whether it contributed substantively to the review question.

An article was included only when it supported all three links below:

1. A mobility, visitation, location, place, or access construct.
2. A consumer, marketing, service, commercial, retail, or local-market phenomenon.
3. A substantive connection with market exposure, localized market opportunity, local conversion conditions, local sales, or transferable theory for spatially heterogeneous local markets.

Retail, store, shopping, foot-traffic, or mobility terminology did not receive automatic priority. These terms indicated possible relevance but did not replace the three-part decision logic.

## Final decisions

Each of the 599 unique records received one final decision: include or exclude. Records used provisionally during calibration were resolved before the final PRISMA-informed counts were produced, leaving no unresolved category in the reported corpus.

Excluded records received one primary reason from `R1` through `R4`, as defined in `exclusion_criteria.md`. The final corpus contained 86 included articles and 513 excluded records.

## Relationship to the integrative synthesis

The retained corpus was organized using the analytical distinctions documented in `../coding_framework/coding_framework.md`. The synthesis preserved the difference between what each study observed and what its evidence could establish about local sales.

Mobility, visits, access, transactions, digital information, and sales were not treated as interchangeable indicators. Instead, the synthesis compared their analytical roles in interpreting when observed consumer mobility may be informative about local sales across places.
