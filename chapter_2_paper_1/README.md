# Chapter 2 / Paper 1: Systematic-Integrative Review

## Consumer Mobility and Local Sales: A Systematic-Integrative Review of What the Evidence Establishes

This directory contains the public review materials for Chapter 2 of *Consumer Mobility as Evidence About Local Markets*. The study examines what existing research establishes about observed consumer mobility and local sales and explains why conclusions change with the consumers represented, the mobility measure, the commercial outcome, the market and period examined, and the research design.

## Research question

> What does existing research establish about the relationship between observed consumer mobility and local sales, and how do market settings, measures, and study designs shape what can be concluded?

## Role in the dissertation

Chapter 2 provides the interpretive foundation for the dissertation by keeping observed consumer mobility, local sales, and localized market opportunity analytically distinct. Consumer mobility records movement across or within physical locations and may be represented through traffic, footfall, visits, proximity, or flows. These observations show that consumers reached a place, but they do not establish demand, purchasing, or sales unless those outcomes are observed separately.

Local sales refer to sales attributed to a focal firm or offering within a specified area and period. A gain for one firm, store, or channel does not necessarily indicate growth in the broader local market because spending may have shifted across competitors, channels, or places. Assessing a localized market opportunity requires additional evidence about buyer needs, available alternatives, channel conditions, and the firm’s ability to serve the market profitably.

The review summarizes what each form of evidence can establish and integrates findings produced for different points between consumer movement and local sales. This comparison explains why mobility may describe or predict commercial activity within a defined setting without showing that observed consumers purchased, that sales increased across the market as a whole, or that an initial result persisted after consumers, firms, and competitors adjusted.

## Review design

The study combines a systematic search and screening process with an integrative comparison of the retained literature. Scopus and Web of Science were searched on May 15, 2026, without a start-date restriction. The search protocol was intentionally broader than the final research question because relevant evidence appears in studies of physical presence, visitation, access, purchasing, spending, sales, competition, and other commercial outcomes.

| Review stage | Records |
|---|---:|
| Records identified in Scopus before additional filters | 1,261 |
| Records identified in Web of Science before additional filters | 574 |
| Records exported from Scopus after database filters | 521 |
| Records exported from Web of Science after database filters | 290 |
| Total exported records | 811 |
| Cross-database duplicates removed | 212 |
| Unique records screened | 599 |
| Records excluded after screening | 513 |
| Articles retained for the integrative synthesis | 86 |

PRISMA 2020 guided the reporting of identification, screening, and inclusion, while the integrative synthesis compared the retained studies without treating heterogeneous measures and designs as estimates of the same relationship.

Each article contributed evidence to at least one of five analytical questions:

1. Who was observed?
2. Why did consumers move, or what process generated the observed movement?
3. Which commercial outcome was measured?
4. Which firms, channels, places, and market boundaries were included?
5. What can the research design establish about prediction, a specific action, change over time, or persistence?

This structure separates what each study observed from what its design allows researchers to conclude.

## Principal conclusions

Across the 86 retained articles, mobility measures described or predicted commercial activity for different consumers, outcomes, markets, and periods, but the studies did not observe the same behavioral or commercial relationship. Some counted passersby, entrants, visitors, repeat visitors, or consumers who had already purchased, while the outcomes included purchase incidence, spending, sales, revenue, and other commercial measures attributed to different firms, channels, places, and geographic levels.

Because these measures represent different people and outcomes, a count of passersby cannot show how many entered or purchased, and a sales gain for one organization cannot show whether total local spending increased. Predictive evidence can establish that mobility helps forecast an outcome within the setting studied, but it does not establish what would happen if a firm changed mobility or another market condition. Evidence of persistence additionally requires observing mobility and commercial outcomes after consumers, firms, and competitors have had time to adjust, a condition that only one retained study directly approximated.

The review provides the interpretive basis for the empirical chapters: Chapter 3 examines the association between consumer mobility and localized sales performance when mobility, sales, and place-based digital sentiment can be aligned, while Chapter 4 examines how multiple mobility indicators characterize the spatial organization of local market access when direct market outcomes are unavailable.

## Public documentation and access limits

The repository provides an auditable record of the search logic, eligibility criteria, aggregate screening results, coding structure, and final included-study inventory. It does not redistribute the commercial Scopus and Web of Science exports or the copyrighted article files examined during the review.

The public materials allow readers to inspect how the corpus was constructed and how the studies were organized for synthesis. The complete interpretation of the evidence, including the comparison of what different measures and designs can establish, remains in Chapter 2 of the dissertation.

Terms such as *exposure*, *opportunity*, and *conversion* remain in the original database queries when they formed part of the search syntax used on May 15, 2026. They are preserved to document the actual search procedure and should not be interpreted as constructs adopted by the final synthesis.
